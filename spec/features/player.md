| PLAY-01 | Negotiate with real device profile, user bitrate/passthrough settings and server defaults, then choose DirectPlay/remux/transcode and tracks. | Use vector decisions, not platform-name capability guesses. Selected method must actually render/decode the source; preserve start and pause intent through negotiated replacements. Show unsupported/error honestly rather than claiming direct play or passthrough. |
| PLAY-02 | Preparation/buffering has explicit Preparing, delayed “Still waiting for playback. You can go back and try again.”, terminal error and Back to details. | A genuinely stalled startup/rebuffer reaches bounded failure (45 s player timeout); Back is usable while loading. Recoverable HLS retry resumes same viewing, position and tracks without unnecessary reopen. Failure exit saves the best observed position and returns to safe detail recovery. |
| PLAY-03 | Player surface OK/Up/Down reveals controls on Play/Pause without toggling; visible Ask-segment surface OK instead skips once. | With no skip offer, first OK only shows controls. Focused button center follows its actual action. Surface Left/Right seek 10 s; after repeat count 5, held direction seeks 30 s steps. Focused control arrows retain normal navigation. |
| PLAY-04 | Play/Pause action label and state update from player intent/events, including system media keys. Controls auto-hide after 10 s idle unless paused, accessible, panel open or actions focused. | Pause pins controls immediately; play resumes timeout. Buffering is not mistaken for paused intent. Play/Pause delivered during track-menu closure, buffering or reopen changes intent once and is not lost/doubled by separate media-session paths. |
| PLAY-05 | Back priority: close chapters, Seek, track chooser; otherwise hide controls with feedback; otherwise exit viewing. During next-episode switch Back requests exit. | Panel closes to its exact opener; Seek Done/Back returns to current-position control with reader acknowledgment. Hidden controls are absent from accessibility. Reader hears Controls hidden on surface, can show controls again, and a second Back exits. |
| PLAY-06 | Timeline displays position/total, seek preview/trickplay and accessible current-position button with Seek panel. | Clock advances visually without tick announcements. Focus/query obtains current position; pause/seek/open update useful spoken state. Unknown/implausible duration is omitted. | PLAY-07 | Pending seeks during replacement preparation accumulate against last intended target and apply when seeking becomes available. | Paused reopen + Right,Right,Left becomes exactly +10 s and stays paused. Clamp to known duration/zero. New player/viewing does not inherit an old pending seek. Server-side seek maintains full-media timeline, A/V sync and external subtitle timing. |
| PLAY-08 | Audio/Subtitles menus list Off plus labeled tracks, selected state, language/codec/channels/name/default/forced/accessibility distinctions. | Disambiguate duplicate labels. “Non-Forced” text does not suppress a true forced badge. | PLAY-09 | Audio route/output changes and decoder/output failures can recover by renegotiating or local audio fallback, retaining intent. | No silence labeled passthrough success; capability snapshot describes current sink, decoder and channel limits. | PLAY-12 | Text and bitmap subtitles fit the displayed video and move above controls, skip/next cards and trickplay without changing video fit. | No clipping at letterbox boundaries, no text-size shrink from reduced safe height, no subtitles under UI. | PLAY-16 | Next episode resolution is chronological, across seasons, filters unusable/current entries and distinguishes pending/failed/none. | Final episode cannot restart itself or an earlier episode; missing optional lookup is not proof of series end. | PLAY-17 | With credible credits, next card uses 10 s countdown; without credits, last-window card shows time to physical end and waits. Play now advances once; Cancel suppresses continuation for viewing. | Paused, buffering, preparing, obscured panels and deliberate seeking suspend early countdown. Physical end moves on once when eligible. Reader enabled disables automatic advance and requires Play now. Cancel removes card and restores focus; end after cancellation exits. |
| PLAY-18 | Manual/countdown next from the credits card and genuine physical end complete watched state exactly once before opening next. Ordinary Stop/Back keeps actual resume. | One viewing produces one Start and one Stop; internal reopens do not. Completion produces Played=true/one play count, episode/season/series/Next up refresh. Repeated callbacks cannot double-complete. Physical EOS, countdown and manual continuation need separate evidence. |
| PLAY-19 | Paused seek/clamp at media end remains paused until explicit Play. | DirectPlay and transcode match: no automatic next episode or natural-end exit merely from seeking to end while paused. Play then completes once. Unreliable file runtime is not fabricated into an end oracle. |
| PLAY-20 | Periodic progress, pause/seek/track changes and final Stop use full-media position and coherent session/track/play-method identity. Final report survives UI disposal. | Vectors define wire semantics; failure to report is visible/recoverable, cleanup stops encoding even after error. Catalog waits for reporting and authoritative refresh. Backgrounding doesn't manufacture natural completion. |
| PLAY-21 | Replacement decoder waits for old release completion; cleanup/network resources are bounded and do not scale per viewing. | Rapid reopens/next and Back while loading remain responsive. No simultaneous vendor decoder on normal completed release path; 5 s bound is recorded as overlap risk, not ordinary success. No leftover transcode after normal app exit. Resource/renderer proof requires relevant device, not only JVM tests. |
| PLAY-22 | External text subtitles prepare and load independently from audio/video. | Publish supported subtitle choices without fetching them. Fetch only the explicitly selected external track; unselected tracks open no connections. Cold extraction, HTTP failures, malformed text and read timeouts never hold READY, rebuffer A/V, move the A/V clock, hold natural end after A/V drains, or fail playback. Keep retrying the selected track without reselection (including extraction taking 242 s); delays are 1, 2, …, 10 s, then 10 s between failed attempts. Off, track change and playback release cancel the old load/retries. Off/on and local seeks reuse parsed cues during the same media period; a replacement may fetch the server's cache. Full-file subtitles retain authored timestamps on a server seek; server-rebased subtitles share the local video origin. Clipping applies to both media and subtitles. Switching between these origins recomputes the text offset. Embedded and burned-in subtitles retain their existing path. |

External-text portability: Apple and web implement PLAY-22's selection, cancellation, retry and clock semantics using their native player/network layers; the Android MediaSource/Loader implementation itself is platform-specific. `external-subtitle-loading` vectors define retry delay and full-file source offset (including large clocks and counts). The loader must not consult the player's 45 s A/V stall deadline. This independence is behavioral and also requires a delayed/failing HTTP fixture test on each platform; vectors alone do not prove readiness or cancellation. Android bounds an external subtitle response to 16 MiB before parsing, treats oversize text as an isolated subtitle load failure, and retries while selected.

Platform-dependent presentation: Apple/web derive their own real codec, HDR, resolution, channel and output capabilities. Do not advertise Android FFmpeg, DTS/TrueHD/AC3 passthrough, PGS local decoding, HDMI refresh behavior or a protocol just because the Android profile supports it. Use server conversion where supported; label its result and burned-in limitations. When neither local decode nor server conversion is available, explain the unsupported format and provide Back/retry. Browsers use available native/HLS facilities and require honest network/auth capability; TV and phone use native media controls. The UI may use native player sheets while retaining these actions, state and recovery semantics.
