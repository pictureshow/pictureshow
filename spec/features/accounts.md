| AUTH-15 | A signed-in viewer on a phone or tablet approves another device's Quick Connect code from Settings → Approve a TV sign-in, with that viewer's own token (`POST /QuickConnect/Authorize?code=`), after checking `/QuickConnect/Enabled`. | AUTH-16 | A saved viewer can have a profile PIN on this device (Settings → Set a PIN), the way Plex profile PINs work: 4 to 8 ASCII digits typed on a number pad (remote D-pad, number keys or touch). Pictureshow asks for it before switching to that viewer from the chooser, and also when Pictureshow opens on it unless the viewer turned "Ask when Pictureshow opens" off. Any route that makes it current without the chooser asks like a switch. Unlocking the next viewer doesn't lock the open one in the moment before the switch lands; leaving it does. Nothing behind the lock composes (rows, deep links, playback entries). The PIN never leaves the device and is saved only as a salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash; the unlock lives only in process memory. Wrong tries count in a row and only the right PIN (or a new PIN) resets the count; a wait ending does not. Four tries are free, then each wrong one waits 30 s, 1 min, 5 min, 15 min and 1 h; at 10 wrong in a row the PIN is off and only the server password (or Quick Connect) opens the viewer, until it sets a new PIN. Waits run on a monotonic clock (Android `elapsedRealtime`), never the wall clock, so changing the date or time can't shorten one; after a restart only time since boot counts, so a restart begins the wait again. Signing in with the password (or Quick Connect) unlocks the viewer, which is the Forgot PIN path. Only the unlocked viewer changes or turns off its PIN, and only after typing the current one (AUTH-19); removing a viewer drops its PIN. Jellyfin keeps the real limits (parental rating, tags, libraries, schedules) and enforces them on every request; the PIN only stops someone on this device opening a viewer they shouldn't. | Vectors `profile-pin-valid-*`, `profile-pin-needed-*`, `profile-pin-attempt-*` (application.json). The count survives each wait; the waits grow 30 s → 1 h; the tenth wrong try locks the PIN out and the right PIN is then refused; a right PIN during a wait is refused and not counted; a restart (monotonic clock below the saved failure time) begins the wait again; non-ASCII digits are refused. |
| AUTH-17 | A 403 from the server is an access refusal, never a wrong password. At sign-in (`Users/AuthenticateByName`) a disabled user and one outside its allowed viewing hours both answer 403 with the same body, so the copy names both: "The server turned this account away. It may be outside its allowed viewing hours, or turned off. Ask your server administrator." In session a 403 (as viewing hours begin) says the server didn't allow this for the account. Wrong password and unknown user stay 401. | Vectors `access-refusal-*` (application.json). OBSERVED on Jellyfin 12 (2026-11-16): disabled and outside-schedule sign-in 403 "Error processing request."; an existing token inside a blocked schedule gets 403 on `/Users/Me` and `/Items`. |
| AUTH-18 | Five minutes or more away from Pictureshow counts as opening it again. The time away is measured from when no Pictureshow screen is visible (Android: the process lifecycle's ON_STOP) to when one is again (ON_START), on the monotonic clock, so changing the date or time can't shorten it, and a restart in between always counts as long enough. Then every unlock, PIN-change permission and password proof is dropped, and the viewer on screen is asked like at a fresh start, following its "Ask when Pictureshow opens" choice. Anything that keeps Pictureshow on screen is not time away: the in-app screensaver, picture-in-picture, a dialog or the notification shade over it. The lock is more than a screen: while a viewer is behind it nothing plays for that viewer. Its music stops at the relock (the position saved, the queue kept), and its media session goes with it, so the notification, a headset or Bluetooth button and Assistant can't resume it. Play is refused until unlock. The device stops being a remote-control target for that viewer and withdraws its listing, and drops any command already on the way. It stops following a Watch-together group, and the open player closes with the screens. A Cast receiver keeps playing, since it is another screen, but can't be resumed from this device. The TV launcher's Watch Next rows show only a viewer past the lock: none while one is behind it, cleared at a relock or a switch to a locked viewer, back once unlocked. A title opened from outside (Watch Next, a link, Assistant) while a viewer is behind its PIN opens only if that same viewer unlocks; a switch from the lock screen drops it. A viewer the lock doesn't ask for (no PIN, or opening question off) carries on. | Vectors `profile-pin-relock-*` (application.json): never left stays open; 4 min and 4 min 59.999 s stay open; exactly 5 min and 1 h lock; a monotonic clock below the saved time (restart) locks. Android `ProfileLockStoreTest` covers the store, `ProfileRelockObserverTest` the lifecycle wiring, and `MusicHostTest`, `RemoteTargetHostTest`, `WatchNextCoordinatorTest` and `PlaybackEntryTest` the playback and launcher behind the lock. |
| AUTH-19 | Changing a PIN, turning it off, or changing "Ask when Pictureshow opens" asks for the current PIN first, even on the open viewer, since a viewer can be open without anyone having typed its PIN (opening question off). Those tries count with unlock tries: same running count, the same waits and the same hard limit (AUTH-16). A right PIN allows one change, and that permission ends unused when the new-PIN screen is cancelled or left or the viewer changes. Setting the first PIN needs none. A password or Quick Connect sign-in allows one change without the old PIN, which is the Forgot PIN path. That proof is one-time like a typed PIN: setting, changing or turning off the PIN, or changing the opening question, uses it up, and it ends when the PIN settings are left, when another viewer becomes current after the signed-in one, or when Pictureshow locks again (AUTH-18), so a parent's sign-in can't leave a window for someone else to turn the PIN off. Cancelling the new-PIN screen keeps it. | Vectors `profile-pin-change-*` (application.json): the first PIN is free; an open viewer alone is not enough; a typed current PIN or a password sign-in allows it. Vectors `profile-pin-proof-*`: a change, leaving the settings, a viewer change or a relock ends the proof; the sign-in landing or a cancelled new-PIN screen keeps it. |

Touch adaptations are specified in [touch.md](touch.md): password Show/Hide, Material forms, dialog dismissal and compact navigation. They retain these account semantics.


## AUTH-10 retained-card port status (2026-11-15)

The retained-card choice is the round-3 desktop decision, reversible for operator review. Contract cases are in
`spec/vectors/account-sign-out.json`; `AccountSignOutVectorsTest` executes the real Kotlin account store through
local persistence, reload, repeated sign-out, sign-in replacement and Remove.

- **Desktop:** uses `AccountStore.signOutKeepingAccount` to clear the token/current selection, search history, home
  preferences, pending preference writes and retired credentials for that viewer while keeping its identity/server
  card. After sign-out, the controller targets that retained card once, even if the chooser was already open. A
  trailing chevron shows it can be activated; choosing it fills the saved username and focuses Password. That username
  metadata clears for another-user/new-server flows, and passwords remain form-local and reset on account changes.
  The controller captures the live credential before clearing local state for best-effort remote logout.
- **Android (TV and touch): ported 2026-11-16 (leg 156).** `HomeViewModel.signOut` calls
  `signOutKeepingAccount`, which now also marks the card `signedOut: true` (the vectors carry the field). A card the
  viewer signed out of keeps identity and server only: `AccountSnapshot.privateStateOwners` excludes it, and every
  private-state registry reads that list (downloads and offline progress in `OfflineEngine`, the saved music queue in
  `MusicHost`, launcher rows and their dismissals in `WatchNextRuntime`). A session the server ended (AUTH-12, `expired`
  without `signedOut`) keeps its downloads for the next sign-in. Sign out asks first on TV (Home chip menu, Settings,
  the button below Home's rows) and touch (Home's account menu), starting on Cancel; the chooser and the touch account
  list show "Signed out · Sign in again" and land on that card, which opens sign-in with the username filled.
  Tests: `OfflineEngineTest`, `MusicHostTest`, `WatchNextCoordinatorTest`, `AccountStoreTest`, `ScreenModelsTest`.
- **Web: port owed.** `web/src/main.tsx` `logout()` filters the viewer out of persisted `accounts` today. It needs
  retained identity/server metadata with an empty token, an unselected session, sign-in-again card/routing and menu
  confirmation. The vector runner lists this suite as owed, rather than claiming a runtime port.
- **Apple (iOS/iPadOS/tvOS): port owed.** The `apple` branch's `apple/App/PictureshowApp.swift` `signOut()` removes
  the viewer from the snapshot before best-effort logout. `AccountsView` already offers Sign in again for an empty
  token; port sign-out persistence, confirmation and the shared cases, and preserve catalog/playback/history cleanup.

The legacy Kotlin `signOut` is gone; web and Apple ports should add the `signedOut` flag so an expired session
(which keeps downloads) and a sign-out (which doesn't) stay distinguishable. `removeAccount` continues to forget a viewer. Source audit above describes the checked code, not
native runtime verification on those other platforms.
